01 The one line

"Game film for traders."

Athletes review their tape to get better. Traders almost never do — and the ones who want to have no coach to do it with. TapeScore is that review layer: it turns a trader's own logged sessions into specific, quantified, mentor-grade feedback.

The name carries the whole idea

Score — from box score, where you check a player's stats. Here you check your own.

Tape — game tape (reviewing your performance), and reading the tape (the live buy/sell order flow). Two trading meanings in one syllable.

02 Who it's for

Serious retail futures traders — with a friendlier front door.

The core user trades index futures intraday (the founder's frame is NQ/MNQ) and speaks fluent RVOL, ADR, ATR, initial balance, R-multiples, EMAs. The product does not apologize for that depth. But it offers a plain-English surface so a less-advanced trader will still journal.

DEFAULT VIEW

Highlights

Plain-English presentation over the exact same deep engine. A verdict sentence, one focus, clean numbers. This is what a new account sees first — it does not remove the sophistication, it consumes it and speaks plainly.

OPT-IN VIEW

Detailed Tape

The fully broken-out instrument — every metric, every tag column, the raw jargon. The graduation, not the front door. Same data, more surface.

Naming principle worth internalizing: name the view, never the user. "Beginner/Pro" was rejected as condescending — "Highlights / Detailed Tape" is on-brand game-film language.

03 The core thesis

It grades your decisions, not your P&L.

This is the single most important sentence in the product, and it must survive contact with a stranger in the first 90 seconds. A green day can score low; a red day can score high. Two ideas do the heavy lifting:

  • Execution vs. Compliance are separate axes — never merged. Execution = did you follow your own entry/exit criteria on each trade. Compliance = did you follow your rules (daily loss limit, size cap, no sizing up after a loss, cooldown, trade cap). This is what separates "good process, bad luck" from "broke discipline, got lucky."
  • Excursion-based coaching. How far price went in your favor (MFE) and against you (MAE), measured in ATR units, plus where price drifted after you exited — these turn raw fills into behavior feedback: are you cutting winners early? Taking too much heat for the profit you get? Exiting at the right time?
04 The loop

What a trader actually does, in order.

The whole product is one daily loop. The nav follows it literally — Prep → Trade → Review — using athlete/verb naming, not app-feature naming.

01PREP

Daily Prep

Read the day before the open — day type, volatility and volume conditions, key levels — and write your mood and trade plans. The AI gives its own read and compares what it sees to what you planned.

02TRADE

Intraday log

Paste a chart screenshot; the AI reads the instrument, entry, stop and target off the bracket orders and auto-tags the setup. You jot why you took it. Screenshot-to-tags is the friction-killer that makes journaling actually happen.

03REVIEW

EOD Recap

Your trades clustered on a chart, MFE/MAE ratios, where price went after each exit, session behaviors, an optional recorded-session AI commentary — then one TapeScore for the day. This is the payoff screen; the post-exit verdicts are the sharpest thing in the product.

04WEEKLY

Weekly review

A coach synthesis of the pattern across days — one theme, one focus for next week. It only earns its place when it says something a single day can't.

05ANALYTICS

Analytics

Performance per setup, confluence, and market condition, so a trader can see which of their edges actually work. Answers first ("what's working / what's costing you"), tables second.

05 The moat

The defensibility is the coaching lens — not the interface.

A competitor can copy these screens in a weekend. What they can't copy is the methodology: a real mentor's framework plus a quantified analytical method, encoded server-side and personalized to each trader's own profile. Design implication: the interface is the delivery vehicle for judgment. It should feel like a coach's read, not a dashboard's readout.

Personalization is mandatory (it's multi-tenant)

Every trader has a profile describing how they trade. The product coaches in that trader's framework and vocabulary — it never imposes one trader's method on another. Concretely for you: surfaces like order-flow tables or initial-balance panels must be able to disappear for a trader whose profile doesn't use them. Design components that gracefully absent themselves, not fixed layouts that assume every field.

06 The vocabulary

The words on every screen.

You don't need to trade to design this, but you need to recognize these terms — they're the nouns of the interface. The golden rule for the UI: a verdict word comes before every raw number. "Bar volatility: very high — 1-min ATR 6.9 (~2.7× normal)," never a bare "ATR 69."

R / R-multiple
Reward measured against the risk taken. The outcome unit — judge sizing and targets in R, not dollars.
MFE
Max favorable excursion — the best point a trade reached in your favor. Low capture of it = cutting winners early.
MAE
Max adverse excursion — the heat taken before it worked. Low MAE on winners = sharp entries.
Capture
How much of the favorable move you actually banked. A decision metric (exit discipline), not raw P&L. Caps at 100%.
Post-exit drift
Where price went 5–15 min after you exited. Tells you whether the exit was right.
RVOL
Relative volume — today's pace vs. normal. "Very quiet" at 25%.
ADR / ATR
Average daily range / average true range — how much the market moves. The volatility regime.
IB (initial balance)
The range of the first hour. Its size vs. the 10-day average frames the day.
Day type
Trend / range / reversal / overnight — the character of the session.
PDH / PDL · IBH / IBL · ONH / ONL
Prior-day, initial-balance, and overnight highs/lows — the key horizontal levels.
Setup
A named, repeatable trade pattern from the trader's own playbook (e.g. break & retest, EMA continuation).
07 Easy to get wrong

Three domain guardrails.

These trip up newcomers to the domain and will trip up UI copy if you don't know them.

  • Getting stopped out is not bad execution. If the idea was invalidated, the stop did its job. Heat (MAE) is a descriptive entry-quality signal, never a penalty. Never let the UI scold a trade for hitting its stop.
  • Order-flow confirmation is a sizing gate, not a validity requirement. "2 of 3 reads" only governs whether you're allowed to size up — it never means a normal-size trade was invalid. Never fail a trade for "low order flow."
  • Small samples must say so. "Win rate 37%" with no sample size is false precision. Every stat carries its n=, and anything too thin is greyed out with "too few to judge." Suppression builds trust — surfacing a confident-looking number off 4 trades destroys it.
08 · start here The visual brief

The look is locked. The reason it's locked matters more than the tokens.

The founder watched another Claude-built trading journal ("Legion Terminal") and it looked strikingly like TapeScore — not because anyone copied, but because AI-assisted design converges on the same "trading terminal" basin: near-black ground, amber-gold accent, thin lucide icons in a left rail, a semicircle loss-limit gauge, a KPI stat-card row, mono green/red numbers. For a product whose whole pitch is "we're different," looking generic is an existential problem. Your north star: a TapeScore screen must be unmistakably TapeScore, not "what an AI builds when you ask for a trading journal."

The convergence trap — what we deliberately walked away from

Amber-on-dark-graphite is itself the attractor. So the escape wasn't a nicer amber — it was leaving the whole basin: a different accent, real typographic hierarchy instead of a card grid, and a signature score mark instead of a stock gauge.

Palette — and the one rule that governs it

Carbon-black cool-neutral ground (not violet, not warm). Scoreboard-blue is the brand and UI accent — and it is used for nothing else. Green, red, and caution-gold carry trading meaning only (P&L, score bands). A designer breaks the system the moment a link turns green or a nav element turns gold. Premium comes from proportion, type, and spacing — never simulated material (silver/chrome/gradients read as gray and add AI-tells).

Type — the wordmark's DNA is the UI's DNA

Archivo for display (this page is set in it), Hanken Grotesk for body. The brand signature is the weight contrast in the wordmark: TapeScore — "Tape" Light, "Score" Bold. Echo that Light/Bold pairing in headings; retire the tiny-uppercase-gray label pattern that's an AI-dashboard tell. Big, confident display numerals are part of the identity.

The TapeScore mark

The score is rendered as a composition ring: one thick ring split into three weighted segments — Risk (50%) · Execution (30%) · Capture (20%). Each segment's length is that component's weight in the score; each fills to its own sub-score; the hue is the band color. It is deliberately not P&L or win-rate — putting money in the mark would break "grade decisions, not P&L." Below, a corrective month reads its own story: Execution short (an entry-timing leak), Capture nearly full (exits held).

The logo (shipped) is a "film frame": a gold film gate with punched sprocket rails framing three ascending bone-white candlesticks, with the Tape-Light / Score-Bold wordmark. Assets live in public/brand/. The candlesticks are a deliberate exception to an otherwise no-chart-iconography rule — they read as the subject, gold frame receding.

Information architecture — finding-first

A screen leads with the analytical finding as a plain-language headline, with the score offset beside it as support — the finding is the star, the number is the credential. Copy states fact, never causality ("Early entries took 1.0R more heat than confirmed entries" — not "...which caused the loss"). Facts come from data; any prose interpretation comes from the AI, and the seam between them stays visible.

Every finding-first screen must handle three states — and the third is the make-or-break one:

Positive

Protect the edge. "Your opening-pullback setup carried the month." Green band, reinforce what works.

Corrective

One fixable leak. "Early entries took 1.0R more heat." Caution band, one AI-suggested adjustment.

Inconclusive

Refuse to invent a lesson. "Quiet month. Nothing separated itself." The hero must not manufacture a finding — honesty over a fake insight.

09 Voice

How the product talks.

  • Verdict before number, everywhere. The word first, the metric in a quiet mono pill behind it.
  • Analytical and mentor-like — direct, specific, quantified. No hype, no filler, no encouragement-for-its-own-sake. Every claim cites the trade or number it came from.
  • Honest about what it doesn't know. "Only 3 IB-fade trades — not enough to conclude" beats a confident-sounding average. The product must never over-promise a fully-formed coach.
  • Ends on one testable next step, phrased as an adjustment the trader can try — not a laundry list.
10 Honest state

Built vs. still maturing.

REAL TODAY

The mechanical richness

MFE/MAE, post-exit drift, execution/compliance scoring, screenshot auto-tagging, per-setup analytics, and the full Prep → Trade → Review → Weekly → Analytics loop all work.

MATURING

The judgment layer

The coach getting smart enough to grade day-type selectivity and give mentor-grade themes. It improves as a trader accumulates data — design shouldn't imply a finished oracle before it's there.
