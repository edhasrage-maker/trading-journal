TapeScore · EOD · Entry efficiency, rethought · Round 1

Your trades work immediately, or they don't.

The current panel compares average MFE to average MAE and calls it entry timing. It can't: losers are capped by the stop and winners aren't, so any trader who uses a stop and lets some winners run reads "sharp" — it's a property of having a stop, not of timing. That's why it says the same thing every day.

This replaces it with the question the data can actually answer: how much heat does a trade take before it pays? Heat is measured as a share of the risk you planned (or in ×ATR when no stop was tagged), bucketed against your own history — so it's style-agnostic, and a 3R trader and a scalper both get a real read. Every number below is yours, from prod, computed 2026-08-18.

The finding this surfaces on your book

Of the 104 trades that never hit their stop: the 46 that stayed under half your planned risk won 89% of the time for +$10,591. The 58 that pushed past half won 43% for +$3,471. A further 76 hit the stop outright. Your winners show up fast — and that is a decision rule, not an adjective.

Design noteswhat changed and why
What it fixes
  • No more structural verdict. MFE>MAE is guaranteed for anyone with a stop; win rate per heat bucket is not.
  • No causal claim. "Your entry timing is sharp" becomes a measured fact with n and $ attached.
  • Not a duplicate. The MFE:MAE ratio already lives in the recap ledger — this section stops repeating it.
  • The give-back line survives — it was the only specific thing in the old panel.
  • Card wrapper → hairline section, per the locked language.
No stop tagged?
  • The axis becomes ×ATR at entry — 98% of your trades carry one vs 78% with a stop, so it always resolves.
  • Same fallback ladder rMultiple() already uses: planned-stop first, ATR second. Nothing new invented.
  • Units never mix in one view. If the day's trades have stops, everything is share-of-risk; if not, everything is ×ATR — the header says which.
  • Neither available → the trade is excluded and counted out loud ("1 trade isn't measurable").
  • Your ATR cliff is even sharper: under 0.25×ATR wins 86%, past it 24%.
Honesty rules baked in
  • The stop-out bucket is marked mechanical and greyed — it can't be evidence, and pretending otherwise is how the old panel got here.
  • Cliff needs n≥40 scored trades; under that, no claim (state ③).
  • Buckets show n and net $, never a bare percentage.
  • Baseline is the trailing book, so the read moves as you change.
  • Open: bucket edges fixed at 25/50/75/100 vs fitted per trader — fixed is more honest, fitted is more useful. Your call.
